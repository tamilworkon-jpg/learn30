<Section id="top-facts" type="topFacts" title="Top facts" minutes={15}>

1. Supervised learning needs labels; foundation models are pre-trained with self-supervised learning on unlabelled data (Day 3).
2. Transfer learning reuses a pre-trained model and adapts it with a small labelled dataset (Day 4).
3. Embeddings are vectors that capture meaning, and chunk overlap keeps ideas from being cut in half (Day 8).
4. Output tokens usually cost more than input tokens, and most organisations start from an existing FM instead of pre-training (Day 9).
5. In tool use the model picks the tool and arguments, and the application or agent runtime executes it (Day 10).
6. Hallucinations are mitigated with grounding (RAG), guardrails, lower temperature, human review and evaluation (Day 11).
7. Amazon Bedrock is the serverless FM API; SageMaker AI gives full control; SageMaker JumpStart deploys pre-trained models in your own account (Day 12).
8. Provisioned throughput is billed hourly for guaranteed capacity and is the usual way to serve a custom model (Day 13).
9. Choose an FM by modality, quality on your task, cost, latency, context window, languages, size, customisation support and licence — the biggest model is not automatically best (Day 15).
10. Low temperature gives focused, repeatable answers; high temperature gives varied, creative answers; max tokens caps length and stop sequences end output early (Day 15).
11. The customisation spectrum, cheapest first, is in-context learning → RAG → fine-tuning → distillation → continued pre-training → pre-training from scratch (Day 15).
12. A prompt has four parts: instruction, context, input data and output indicator (Day 16).
13. Zero-shot gives no examples, single-shot one and few-shot several, and none of them changes the model's weights (Day 16).
14. Chain-of-thought prompting asks the model to reason step by step and improves maths, logic and multi-step answers (Day 16).
15. Amazon Bedrock Prompt Management stores prompt templates with variables, model and inference settings, and creates immutable versions (Day 16).
16. Prompt injection hides instructions in user input; indirect injection hides them in documents, web pages or tool results the model reads (Day 17).
17. Jailbreaking bypasses safety rules, hijacking redirects the task and poisoning corrupts training, fine-tuning or RAG data (Day 17).
18. Amazon Bedrock Guardrails offers content filters (including prompt attacks), denied topics, word filters, sensitive-information filters, contextual grounding checks and Automated Reasoning checks, on input and output (Day 17).
19. RAG retrieves relevant chunks of your own data at question time, so answers are current, grounded and citable without retraining (Day 18).
20. Bedrock Knowledge Bases is managed RAG: connect a data source, choose chunking and an embedding model, pick a vector store, then call Retrieve or RetrieveAndGenerate (Day 18).
21. Exam vector stores: OpenSearch Service (search plus vectors), Aurora or RDS for PostgreSQL with pgvector, Neptune for graphs and DocumentDB for JSON documents (Day 18).
22. Continued pre-training uses unlabelled domain text to teach vocabulary and knowledge; fine-tuning uses labelled prompt–response pairs to teach a task, style or format (Day 19).
23. RLHF has humans rank responses, trains a reward model on those preferences and then tunes the FM with reinforcement learning (Day 19).
24. Distillation trains a small student model on a large teacher model's answers for cheaper, faster inference (Day 19).
25. PEFT methods such as LoRA train only small adapters instead of all the weights (Day 19).
26. ROUGE measures recall-oriented overlap for summaries, BLEU measures precision-oriented n-gram overlap for translation and BERTScore measures semantic similarity with embeddings (Day 20).
27. Amazon Bedrock Model Evaluation offers automatic, human (your own work team) and LLM-as-a-judge evaluations, plus RAG evaluation with Knowledge Bases (Day 20).
28. Evaluate RAG in two parts — retrieval (context relevance and coverage) and generation (faithfulness, correctness, citations) (Day 20).
29. Benchmark datasets compare models on standard tasks, but your own representative test set shows fitness for your use case (Day 20).
30. Business fit is judged by task completion rate, user satisfaction, cost per interaction and impact on KPIs (Day 20).

<Section id="traps" type="traps" title="Exam traps" minutes={5}>

- **"Lower the temperature to fix wrong company facts"** — temperature changes randomness, not knowledge. Missing or outdated facts need better context: RAG or prompt content.
- **"Few-shot prompting fine-tunes the model"** — examples in the prompt change no weights and are sent with every request. "Without training" or "least effort" → few-shot; "permanently adapt with thousands of examples" → fine-tuning.
- **"Fine-tune on data that changes every week"** — current or proprietary knowledge, fewer hallucinations and citations point to RAG. Fine-tuning is for style, tone, format or task behaviour.
- **"Poisoned knowledge-base documents are prompt injection"** — corrupting training or RAG data is poisoning; jailbreaking beats safety rules; hijacking replaces the task.
- **"Unlabelled domain text means fine-tuning"** — unlabelled text → continued pre-training; labelled pairs → fine-tuning; human rankings → RLHF; teacher answers → distillation.
- **"ROUGE and BLEU understand synonyms"** — they count word overlap. When meaning matters more than exact words, use BERTScore.
- **"Guardrails only check the user's prompt"** — Bedrock Guardrails apply to both prompts and responses, and a layered defence also needs least privilege, output filtering, human review and logging.
- **"The biggest model is always the best choice"** — pick the smallest model that meets quality, latency and cost requirements.
