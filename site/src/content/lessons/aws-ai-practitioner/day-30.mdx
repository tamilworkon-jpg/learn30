Today's main event is Mock 2: another 65 new questions in 90 minutes, under the same conditions as Mock 1. That means one sitting, no notes, answer everything, and flag and return. Sit it only after you have worked through the Day 29 remediation loop.

Mock 3 is an optional final retake. Use it 1–2 days later, after today's remediation, as your last readiness check before exam day.

**Readiness target:** 80% or more (52 of 65) on your final mock. If you score between 70% and 80%, do one more remediation loop and then sit Mock 3. If you score under 70% on Mock 2, think about moving your exam date back by a few days. Your Pearson VUE booking can usually be rescheduled, but check the current deadlines in the candidate guide.

#### Final-pass checklist: look-alike pairs

- **Bedrock vs SageMaker AI**: managed API access to foundation models vs full control to build, train and host your own models (Day 12).
- **RAG vs fine-tuning vs continued pre-training**: current data added at query time vs a new task or style from labelled examples vs domain vocabulary from unlabelled text (Days 18–19).
- **On-demand vs batch vs provisioned throughput**: pay per token vs discounted offline jobs vs reserved hourly capacity (Day 13).
- **Temperature vs top-p**: how random the next token is vs how much of the probability mass is sampled (Day 15).
- **ROUGE vs BLEU vs BERTScore**: recall for summaries vs precision for translation vs meaning similarity from embeddings (Day 20).
- **Precision vs recall**: avoiding false positives vs avoiding false negatives (Day 6).
- **Overfitting vs underfitting**: high variance vs high bias (Day 2).
- **Guardrails vs Clarify vs Model Cards**: filtering prompts and responses vs detecting bias and explaining predictions vs documenting a model (Days 23–24).
- **Config vs CloudTrail vs Inspector vs Artifact**: configuration compliance vs API activity log vs vulnerability scanning vs AWS's own compliance reports (Day 27).
- **Shared responsibility**: AWS secures the infrastructure; you secure identities, data, configuration and how outputs are used (Day 25).

<Section id="exam-strategy" type="examStrategy" title="Exam-day strategy" minutes={10}>

#### Before the exam

- **Registration**: sign in to your AWS Certification account and register for the **AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01)** exam. You then schedule it with **Pearson VUE**. Check the current price, available languages and any accommodation options when you book. For example, non-native English speakers can ask for extra time, so check the current candidate guide for how and when to request it.
- **Delivery**: choose a **Pearson VUE test centre** or **online proctoring** from home or the office.
  - *Test centre*: arrive early with the identification the candidate guide requires. Personal items such as your phone and watch are stored outside the testing room.
  - *Online proctoring*: run the system test on the computer, webcam and network you will use, well before exam day. On the day, check in early. The check-in includes photos of your ID and your room, and a room scan. You need a quiet, private room with a clear desk and nobody else present. You cannot use paper notes, a second monitor or a phone during the exam.
- **ID and rules**: the exact ID rules, check-in window, break policy and reschedule or cancellation deadlines can change. Read the current AWS Certification candidate guide and your Pearson VUE confirmation email a few days before the exam.

#### Format and scoring

- **65 questions in 90 minutes.** 50 questions are scored and 15 are unscored. The unscored questions are not marked, so treat every question as if it counts.
- **Question types**: multiple choice (one correct answer out of four), **multiple response** (two or more correct answers out of five or more; the question says how many to choose), **ordering** (put 3–5 steps in the correct order) and **matching** (match 3–7 pairs). In multiple response, ordering and matching questions you must get every part right to earn credit.
- **Scoring**: your result is a scaled score from 100 to 1000, and **700 passes**. The score is compensatory: you pass on your total score and do not need to pass each domain separately.
- **No penalty for guessing**: an unanswered question is marked wrong, so answer every question.

#### Pacing and tactics

- **Pace**: 90 minutes ÷ 65 questions ≈ 83 seconds each. Aim for about 75 seconds so one full pass takes about 80 minutes and leaves 10 minutes for review. Pacing checkpoints: question 20 by about 25 minutes, question 40 by about 50 minutes, question 65 by about 80 minutes.
- **Flag and return**: if a question is not clear after about 2 minutes, eliminate what you can, choose your best answer, **flag it** and move on. Never leave it blank. Return to flagged questions in the review time at the end.
- **Read the last line first**: qualifiers such as *most cost-effective*, *least operational overhead*, *most secure* or *choose TWO* decide between two plausible answers.
- **Eliminate first**: drop answers that name out-of-scope services, use absolute wording ("always", "guarantees", "eliminates") or solve a different problem.
- **Change answers only for a reason**: in review, change an answer only when you spot a misread or remember a specific fact. Do not change it on a vague feeling.
- **Stay calm**: if you meet a run of hard questions, remember that some are unscored. Flag them, keep your pace and move on.

#### After the exam

Your pass or fail result and score report appear in your AWS Certification account. They are often available soon after the exam, but can take a few business days. The certification is valid for three years. If you do not pass, the score report shows which domains to work on. Retakes have a waiting period, so check the current policy, then re-run the remediation loop on those domains.
